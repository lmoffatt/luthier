# 3. The kernel

Chapter 3 of the specification, draft 0, 2026-10-12. Sources: `docs/spec/04-kernel.md` at d12b554
(sections 1 to 4 and 6), `src/luthier/syntax.py`, `evaluator.py`, `machine.py`,
`src/luthier/core.lut` and `src/luthier/lib/kernel_laws.lut` at 3445ad8, and the chat decision
recorded as D-2026-10-12-x. Written by Claude Sonnet 5.5. It realizes L7 (the core is minimal and
its programs are data), L14 (what can be written in Luthier is written in Luthier), L2 (holes) and
L3 (levels of equality). Notation is `spec 1`; the translation of sugar into the kernel is
`spec 2`.

## §1 Acts

**3.S1. Three acts.** [implemented] A program is a set of top-level acts with no order. A
declaration `x : T` introduces `x` with its base `T` and no body, which is a hole (L2.1). A
definition `x : T := t`, or `x := t` filling an earlier declaration, gives `x` a body. An alias
`x = t` makes `x` stand for `t`; it is expanded before checking and has no states.

**3.S2. Bodies.** [decided D-2026-10-12-ab] The body of a definition is a term, a `data` term (§2), or
`extern "target"` (§3). `primitive "..."` and `axiom "..."` are applications of names of core
(3.S39), what they mean and where they are allowed is `spec 11`.

**3.S3. Directives.** [decided D-2026-10-12-ab] Directives are not definitions and have no states.
`import` (`spec 12`) and `operator` (`spec 4`) are acts of `luthierSyntax` (`spec 2.S9`); `test`
(`spec 7`) and `cite` (`spec 6`) are functions (3.S39); `gen` and `profile` are gone (`spec 2.S19`).

## §2 Type formers

The kernel has the formers below. Each is a statement with its status.

| former | anonymous | with names | introduction | elimination |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| universe | `U` | | every type | |
| exponential (Pi) | `A -> B` | `(x : A) -> B`, `B` may mention `x` | `fn x { t }` | application `f a` |
| product (Sigma) | `A * B` | `(x : A) * (y : B)`; n-ary `{ x : A, y : B }` | `(a, b)`; `{ x := a, y := b }` | pattern `(x, y)` in `match`; projection `e.x` |
| sum | `A + B` | `{ x : A \|\| y : B }`, n-ary (D-2026-10-13-w) | `x a`, by label | `match` on labels |
| empty | `Empty` | | none | `match e { }`, at any type |
| unit | `Unit` | | `unit` | none needed |
| subtype | `{ x : T \| p }` | `{ x : A, y : B \| p }` | a term of `T` with `p` holding (an obligation) | a term of the subtype is a term of `T` |
| recursive structure | `data { c1 : ... ; c2 : ... }` | constructors are named | the constructors | `match` on constructors, with recursion |
| primitives | `Int`, `Real`, `Float64`, `Rational`, `String` | | literals | `spec 11` |

**3.R1. Names of positions are binders.** [implemented] In the named forms of the exponential,
the product and the sum, the names are binders like the parameter names of a function and are not
part of the identity of the structure. `{ n : Int, b : Bool }`, `(n : Int) * (b : Bool)` and
`Int * Bool` are one structure. Positions are ordered: swapping two components gives a different,
isomorphic structure, and a program that passes one for the other is rejected. The reading where
labels are the identity is a translation, not an equality (L3.1).
Files: k_named_product.accept.lut, k_label_order.reject.lut.
[decided D-2026-10-13-x] In the language designed the names of a record and the labels of a sum are
its projections and its injections, the arrows of the product and of the coproduct, so they are
part of the structure: the kernel term keeps them (`Record`, `RecordValue`, `Proj`, `Sum`, `Inj` in
`src/luthier/lib/term.lth`) and the hash covers them; a record and the bare product of its types are
isomorphic structures, not one. `kernel_laws.lth` states them as named products and coproducts.

**3.R2. A sum is identified by shape.** [implemented] A sum of two units with any labels is the
same structure as `Bool`, since labels are binders; a value of such a sum is accepted where a
`Bool` is expected. `Bool` itself is a sum of two units, defined in `core.lut`.
Files: k_bool_is_sum.accept.lut, k_label_order.reject.lut.

**3.R3. A `data` is freely generated by named constructors.** [decided D-2026-10-13-o] A `data` definition names its
constructors, each with its full type; recursive or not, it is freely generated: distinct
constructors give distinct values and every constructor is injective, by construction and not by
axiom. A sum is written as a `data` without recursion (`Bool`). Freedom says nothing of what a
constructor means: that comes from a law stating its role (`spec 4.R3`, D-2026-10-13-p); a `data`
whose constructors have none states no concept and becomes an alias of a sum.
`v0.1-final` required a recursive constructor and took a non-recursive sum as an alias.
Files: r4_recursive_data.accept.lut.

**3.S4. Anonymous sums and products.** [implemented] `A + B` is `{ left : A || right : B }`.
Sums and products of more than two components associate to the right, so `A * B * C` is
`A * (B * C)` and `(a, b, c)` is `(a, (b, c))`. An alternative of type `Unit` is injected by its
label alone: in `{ true : Unit || false : Unit }`, `true` is `true unit`. Examples: `k_sum.accept.lut`,
`k_union.accept.lut`.

**3.S5. Dependence.** [implemented] In `(x : A) -> B` and `(x : A) * (y : B)` the second part may
mention `x`. A sum has no dependent form: a value holds one alternative only, so none can depend on
another. Example: `k_dependent_product.accept.lut`.

**3.S6. The subtype.** [implemented] `{ x : T | p }` has the inhabitants of `T` for which `p`
holds. Here `p` has `x` free and is a term of type `Bool` or `Law` (§3). A term of `T` used
where the subtype is expected creates an obligation (`spec 4`); a term of the subtype is accepted
where `T` is expected with no marker, the forgetting being implicit. The subtype is primitive
although derivable: the criterion for the kernel's presentation is that the rules of `spec 4` state
directly on it, and minimality is not the criterion.

**3.S7. Empty and unit.** [implemented] `Empty` has no introduction and an elimination `match e { }`
at any type; `Unit` has the one value `unit`. Both are defined in `core.lut` as `data`, which 3.R3
allows since D-2026-10-13-o. Example: `k_empty_unit.accept.lut`. [decided D-2026-10-13-t] Their constructors'
roles (4.R3) are those of objects of the category of 3.S8: `Unit` is terminal, one map into it from
every type, and `Empty` initial, one map out of it to every type; `Bool` has `not`, `and`, `or` and
the laws of a Boolean algebra, `Nat` the laws of its operations, and `List` is the free monoid
(`nil` the unit of `append`, which is associative; `fold` gives `cons` its role, 3.S36).
`kernel_laws.lth` states them.

**3.S8. The kernel as a category.** [implemented] With these formers the kernel is a bicartesian
closed category (products, sums, exponentials, a terminal and an initial structure) with the
dependent forms of the product and the exponential, recursive structures and subtypes. Where the
older source says "type" for a kernel type, this chapter writes "structure" (D-2026-10-12-l);
"type" stays where any type is meant. [decided D-2026-10-13-aa] Version 0 has one universe with
`U : U`, a known limit: it is inconsistent as a logic (Girard's paradox), harmless for testing,
which proves nothing; a hierarchy of universes waits for the layer of proofs (3.S11).

## §3 Terms

**3.S9. Names.** [implemented] A defined name stands for its body. A declared name is a hole and
does not reduce (L2.1).

**3.S10. Match.** [implemented] `match t { p1 -> t1 ; ... }` has one branch per constructor or
label of the type of `t`, none repeated and none missing, unless a variable or `_` branch takes the
rest. A pattern is a constructor or label applied to patterns, a tuple `(p, q)`, a literal, a
variable, or `_`. The checker has no coverage predicate yet: nothing rejects a repeated or a missing branch.

**3.S11. Recursion and the recursor.** [implemented] A definition with a declared type may mention
itself, and definitions may mention each other. Recursion is general: nothing guarantees
termination, and evaluation runs under a step budget (fuel). The recursor of a `data` is a library
definition written with `match` and recursion, the fold of the structure; `k_recursor.accept.lut`
defines it for the natural numbers. A proof layer that made it primitive and forbade general
recursion is not part of version 0 [decided D-2026-10-12-ah].

**3.S12. Laws as terms.** [implemented] A law has type `Bool` or `Law`, a function into
one of these whose parameters are quantified, or a conjunction `p & q`. A law into `Failures E`
(the failures a check finds, spec 5.S9) is taken by the checker (`verdict_type` in `check.py`). What a law is and how laws combine is `spec 5`; this
chapter keeps only that a law is a term of one of those types. `Law` is the type of what a test
runs (`spec 7`). A law may also be valued in the cost type of `spec 5.S2`, which the interpreter does not have yet.

**3.S13. Extern.** [implemented] `extern "python:module.function"` is a value the host provides,
with the type its context gives it; `extern provisional "..."` is the same value marked as debt.
Its validation is `spec 10`, and the rule that it needs a declared type is `spec 4.R5`.
Examples: `r5_extern_value.accept.lut`, `r5_extern_provisional.accept.lut`. Replaced: 3.S39, 3.S40.

## §4 Reduction

Evaluation reduces terms by five rules, each a definitional equality (`≡`, `spec 1`). The programs
named are those of `tests/spec/` that contain the redex; the suite runs them through the checker,
and reduction proper is exercised by `tests/test_kernel.py`. A rule of evaluation counts as implemented
only with a program whose value is compared (D-2026-10-12-ah); 3.S14 to 3.S18 name those of
`tests/eval/` (run by `luthier eval` in `test_elaborate.py::test_eval`) and of `tests/run/` (run by
`luthier run` in `test_run.py`).

**3.S14. Beta.** [implemented] `(fn x { t }) a ≡ t[a/x]`. In `k_recursor.accept.lut` the lambda
`fn k r { succ r }` is applied by `recN`; `tests/eval/lambda.lut` computes 5 by it.

**3.S15. Delta.** [implemented] A defined name reduces to its body. Aliases are expanded before
checking and never reach evaluation. [decided D-2026-10-13-aa] A defined name in head position is
unfolded; a term reduction cannot advance is stuck, the same for every specimen, and folding a
name back for display is the printer's (in `v0.1-final` a recursive function on an argument that
did not reduce stayed folded, `evaluator.py`, which made the normal form depend on the specimen). Programs: `k_recursor.accept.lut`, with the unfolding of `recN` and `add`; `tests/eval/factorial.lut` (24) and `tests/eval/arithmetic.lut`.

**3.S16. Iota.** [implemented] A `match` on a constructor, a label injection or a pair reduces to
the matching branch with pattern variables substituted. Programs: `k_sum.accept.lut`,
`k_union.accept.lut`, `k_empty_unit.accept.lut`, `k_recursor.accept.lut`; and with their values
compared, `tests/eval/data.lut` (2) and `tests/eval/factorial.lut` (24).

**3.S17. Prim.** [implemented] Arithmetic and comparisons on literals of `Int`, `Float64` and
`String`, and `==` on values of structures with structural equality (`spec 4.R8`). Program with
its value compared: `tests/eval/arithmetic.lut` (19); `k_union.accept.lut` (arithmetic on
`Float64`) and `k_dependent_product.accept.lut` (comparisons on `Int`) are only checked.

**3.S18. Extern.** [implemented] An extern applied to as many closed arguments as its type has
parameters calls the host and converts the result back (`spec 10`). No k program of `tests/spec/`
reduces one, and `r5_extern_value.accept.lut` only checks one; the programs of `tests/run/`
(`read_first_line.lut`, `table_cell.lut`, `record_externs.lut`, `vector_norm.lut`) run them, with
their output compared in `test_run.py`.

**3.S19. Terms with holes.** [implemented] A term that contains a hole where the reduction needs a
value does not reduce.

**3.S20. Equality in the kernel.** [implemented] As L3.2 states, binding is exact and
reduction depends on no tolerance (L3.2). Definitional equality is the reduction of this section;
the other levels of equality belong to the object (L3.1, `spec 5`).

## §5 Primitive versus derived, native versus extern

**3.S21. Two senses of primitive.** [implemented] A structure is semantically primitive when the
kernel has it: the formers of §2 and the primitives of `spec 11`. Every other structure is defined
natively from them (`spec 4.R5`). A value is primitive for performance when it is an extern, computed
by the host because it is faster there. A numpy array is not a primitive structure: with
`Fin n = { …}` of `spec 11`, a vector of length `n` is the function type
`Fin n -> Float64`, a matrix is `Fin m -> Fin n -> Float64`, and a numpy array is a value of that
type. Nothing is converted and nothing about the representation needs certifying. Example:
`n5_arrays.accept.lut`.

A value of a function type with a finite domain can be a Luthier lambda or a host object. Four
rules govern the bridge.

**3.S22. Bridge 1, applying a host object.** [implemented] If `v : Fin n -> A` came from the host,
`v i` evaluates as `v[i]`. The interpreter checks `0 <= i < n` itself, since that is the obligation
of `Fin n` and the host would accept `v[-1]` silently.

**3.S23. Bridge 2, passing a lambda to the host.** [implemented] A host function that expects an
array and receives a lambda over `Fin n` gets the array of its `n` values: the interpreter
evaluates the lambda at every index (it materializes it), possible only because the domain is
finite. The cost is `n` evaluations.

**3.S24. Bridge 3, representation by type.** [implemented] `Fin n -> Float64` is a one-dimensional
ndarray, `Fin m -> Fin n -> Float64` a two-dimensional one, `Fin n -> A` for a non-numeric `A` a
list, and a vector of unknown length `(n : Int) * (Fin n -> Float64)` is the pair
`(arr.shape[0], arr)`. The shape is checked when the value crosses: an array declared of length 3
must have shape `(3,)`.

**3.S25. Bridge 4, each entry once.** [implemented] A lambda over `Fin n` is a table: each entry is
computed at most once, as each argument is computed once under call by need. The semantics does not
change, since functions are pure. Without it an iterative algorithm written with lambdas
recomputes its whole history at each read.

**3.S26. Observational equality across the bridge.** [proposed] A lambda and an array of one
function type are compared point by point over `Fin n` (`spec 4.R8`); the domain is finite, so the
comparison is exhaustive.

**3.S27. The order of a sum is part of its rounding.** [implemented] `sumTo n f` is
`f 0 + (f 1 + (... + (f (n - 1) + 0.0)))`, the fold over the additive monoid of `Float64`, which
associates to the right. Written the other way it differs in the last bits. The order belongs to the
definition of the function, and any specimen of it keeps the order (`spec 9`).

**3.S28. What is certified at the bridge.** [implemented] What needs certifying is that the
operations the host provides satisfy the laws that characterize them (`spec 4.R10`). A function may
be given natively, as an extern, or both; each is a model of the same laws. Giving both adds a
corollary, that the two agree, which tightens the tolerance. The libraries name such laws `Agrees`,
a local name; the two implementations are two specimens of one theory (`spec 9`).

## §6 Programs as data

**3.S29. Term and Program as data.** [implemented] [decided D-2026-10-12-x] `Term`, `Pattern`, `Act` and
`Program` are `data`, so that a program is a value of Luthier (L7.4, L14.2). They are written in
`src/luthier/lib/term.lth`, with their model in `term_model.lut`, and `kernel.py` and `term.py`
are the host's. The constructors:
[decided D-2026-10-13-aa] As written in `src/luthier/lib/term.lth` §1, which this item now follows:
`Term` has `Var`, `Lit` (a `Constant`: integer, double or string), `Universe` (for `U`, a keyword),
`World`, `Lam`, `App`, `Pi`, `Sigma`, `Pair`, `Match`, `Inj`, `Sum`, `Data`, `Subtype`, `Extern`,
`Primitive`, `Axiom`, `Hole`, `Record`, `RecordValue`, `Proj` and `Let` (a local definition, which a
block elaborates to); `Pattern` has `PVar`, `PWild`, `PCon`, `PPair`, `PLit`, `PInj`; `Act` has
`Declare`, `Define` and `Datum`: aliases are expanded before checking and leave no act, imports are
resolved into qualified names, and the directives are functions (3.S39, spec 2.S21).

| `data` | constructors |
|---|---|
| `Term` | `Var`, `Lit`, `Universe`, `World`, `Lam`, `App`, `Pi`, `Sigma`, `Pair`, `Match`, `Inj`, `Sum`, `Data`, `Subtype`, `Extern`, `Primitive`, `Axiom`, `Hole`, `Record`, `RecordValue`, `Proj`, `Let` |
| `Pattern` | variable, wildcard, constructor, pair, literal, injection (`PVar`, `PWild`, `PCon`, `PPair`, `PLit`, `PInj` in `term.lth`) |
| `Act` | declaration, definition and datum (`Declare`, `Define`, `Datum`) |
| `Program` | a list of `Act` |

In `Term`, `Var` is a name, `Lit` a literal, `U` the universe, `Lam` and `App` the exponential,
`Pi` and `Sigma` the dependent forms, `Pair` the product introduction, `Match` the eliminator,
`Inj` and `Sum` the sum, `Data` a recursive structure, `Subtype` the subtype, `Extern`,
`Primitive` and `Axiom` the three provided bodies, and `Hole` a declaration with no body. The
constructors of `Pattern` and the fields of `Act` are as listed [decided D-2026-10-12-ah]; D-2026-10-12-x named the
four data and not their fields.

**3.S30. Term is the kernel term.** [implemented] [decided D-2026-10-12-ah] `Term` is the kernel term after elaboration, with the
sugar of `spec 2` translated away, not the surface term. The hashes of the certificates (`spec 6`)
are computed over it.

**3.S31. Names are strings.** [implemented] [decided D-2026-10-12-ah] Variables and labels are strings. Alpha-equivalence, two
terms equal up to renaming of bound variables, is a law of the theory of `Term` (L3.3), and not a
property of a representation by de Bruijn indices.

**3.S32. Quote and eval.** [decided D-2026-10-12-ah] `quote { t }` gives the `Term` of the term `t` with no
obligation. `eval A t : Maybe A` checks `t` against `A` at evaluation time and gives `nothing` when
it does not check. `quote` elaborates to an application of the name `quote` (`elaborate.py`) and `kernel.eval_at` evaluates a quoted term at a type (`test_eval_computes`, `test_eval_constants_and_checks`); `eval` as a core name of type `Maybe A` is not a program yet.

**3.S33. Reduction as a function.** [implemented] [decided D-2026-10-12-ah] The rules of §4 (beta, delta, iota, prim, extern)
are stated as a function `reduce : Term -> Term` in `lib/term.lth`, the interpreter written in
Luthier, certified against the host's evaluator as two specimens of one theory (L14.3, `spec 9`).
[decided D-2026-10-13-aa] As written in `term.lth` §5: the rules are stated on one weak-head step,
`step : Program -> Term -> Maybe Term` (`nothing` when the term is in weak head normal form), and
evaluation under fuel (3.S11) is its iteration, `reduce`, a definition; so running out of fuel is
not a verdict of any law. The model is `term_model.lut`, the host's evaluator `kernel.py` a second
specimen. The strategy is weak head: the head of an application, the scrutinee of a match and the
record of a projection step first, and nothing steps under a binder.

**3.S34. Canonical form.** [implemented] [decided D-2026-10-12-ah] The canonical form of a `Term` renames bound variables
canonically and removes module prefixes. The hashes of `spec 6` are computed over this form, so two
programs that differ in the names of their bound variables or in the module that holds a definition
have one hash.

**3.S35. No self-modification.** [decided D-2026-10-12-x] A `Program` value is data. Evaluating
cannot change the running program: defining is an action on the world, and an action on a world
belongs to `spec 10` (L7.4, L4).

## §7 The kernel's theory in Luthier

**3.S36. `lib/kernel_laws.lth`.** [implemented] The file states the laws every structure of the
kernel satisfies by construction: `Beta`, `PairFst`, `PairSnd` and `Pairing` for the exponential
and the product; `IfTrue`, `IfFalse`, `CaseNothing` and `CaseJust` for sums (stated on `Bool` and
`Maybe`); `FoldNeutral`, `FoldInject` and `FoldCombine` for the recursion principle of `List`, the free monoid. Its models are in
`kernel_models.lut`, and `tests/test_kernel.py` checks `kernel.py` against them: the reduction of
the host is thereby a specimen of this theory (L14.3).
[decided D-2026-10-12-ak] The laws of a structural `==` (reflexive, symmetric, transitive,
substitutive), which make a concrete type an object, are to be stated in the same file.
[decided D-2026-10-13-z] `kernel_laws.lth` §1 states them: `Object` is reflexive, symmetric and
transitive; `Structural` adds substitutivity over every function, which only a structural `==`
has; an observational equality is an `Object` respected by its theory's operations as congruences
(`AlphaCongruent` in `term.lth`).
[decided D-2026-10-13-aa] `src/luthier/lib/kernel_laws.lth` states the kernel's structures as
theories: an object with its equality (§1), terminal and initial objects (`Unit`, `Empty`), a Boolean
algebra (`Bool`), the free monoid (`List`), an ordered commutative semiring (`Nat`), the
exponential, the product and the coproduct, and records and labeled sums as named products and
coproducts. `Beta`, `PairFst`, `PairSnd`, `Pairing` are laws of those theories; `IfTrue`, `IfFalse`,
`CaseNothing`, `CaseJust` are the coproduct's `CaseLeft` and `CaseRight` at the models `ifThenElse`
and `maybes` of `kernel_models.lut`; the roles of the core's constructors are these models
(D-2026-10-13-t).

**3.S37. What it cannot state.** [implemented] Eta, `fn y { f y } == f`, is not statable, since
equality of functions is not structural (`spec 4.R8`); observed pointwise it is beta. Functions over
`Fin n` are not enumerated by `exhaustive`. Both are O11. The recursion principle is stated over
lists with the algebra fixed, until functions of two arguments have generators.

**3.S38. `lib/term.lth`.** [implemented] [decided D-2026-10-12-ah] The theory of `Term`: the data of §6, alpha-equivalence, the
canonical form of 3.S34, substitution, and `reduce` of 3.S33 with the laws that relate it to
`kernel_laws.lth`.
[decided D-2026-10-13-aa] Written as `src/luthier/lib/term.lth`, with its models in `term_model.lut`.


## §8 Constructs as names, and when a file is read

**3.S39. Bodies and directives as names of core.** [decided D-2026-10-12-ab] `extern`, `primitive`
and `axiom` are names of `core.lut`; a body is their application, recognized by the checker, so the
restriction of `primitive` and `axiom` to core and the libraries becomes a law over `Program` (`spec
4 §9`). The directives `test`, `cite` and `import` are functions (`spec 7`, `spec 6`, `spec 12`).

**3.S40. An extern is a route.** [implemented] [decided D-2026-10-12-ac] An extern is a definition like any other:
its name, its declared type and the route to the host's function, a datum. Reading the host's file
is the evaluation of that node, done when a demand reaches it, once per plan, with the hash of what
was read entering the identity of the result (L5.6); a file no demand reaches is never read, and it
need not exist when the program is checked (L11.7). `run.py` reads it on demand and `check.py`
imports no host module (`test_unused_extern_is_not_resolved`, `test_extern_in_a_record_is_read_when_projected`).
