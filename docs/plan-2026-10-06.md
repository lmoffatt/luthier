Terms used throughout. A *law* is a Bool-valued definition or one conjunct of a subtype's predicate
(`05 §0`). An *obligation* is a law applied to a definition, what `luthier test` evaluates. A
*certificate* (`.cert.json`) records the outcome of each obligation with the hash of its code, its
Python and the interpreter. A *generator* draws the values of a law's parameters. *Session A* is the
measurement session of the folder `2026-09-07`: five microphones (mic1 to mic5) and five speaker
positions (parlante_A1 to parlante_A5), with distances measured by tape and dictated as voice notes.
*Sigma* is the standard deviation of a tape distance in the fitted model. *LM* is Levenberg-Marquardt,
the optimizer. The *PIT* of a residual is its probability integral transform: the residual passed
through the distribution function of the fitted model, uniform on [0, 1] when the model is right.

## Part 2. Analysis

### 2.1 Six roots

The thirty-three problems have six roots. Fixing a root fixes its problems together; fixing a
problem alone leaves the root to produce the next one.

**Root 1. The generator is implicit, owned by the type, with structural defaults and fixed budgets,
and the certificate records neither which generator drew nor how many cases it evaluated.** This is
P6, P8, P9, P12, P13, P14, P25, P27, and the lesson of P11. A rejection sampler with a fixed budget
spends 14 minutes failing to draw an `Objective` (P8) because nothing lets a test say "take the
objectives of this program" (P6); a law passes on 24 cases of 100 and the certificate says 100
(P12); tolerances are drawn on (0, 16] because the default knows nothing of what a tolerance is
(P13). `08-tests.md` is the specification of the fix: generators as named values and functors (G1
to G4), numbers with bounds in the type and a metric in the generator (G7), a census of models for
functions and theories (G9), budgets as arguments (G12), certificates keyed by their binding with
counted cases (C1 to C3), and drawing a type as a static error (G5).

**Root 2. Types say less than the code assumes.** `Theta : U` where a vector is meant (P2, P24);
`lmTol` with one bound where it has two (P11, P26); `negate` typing bare functions as a lawful
`Objective` (P1); checks whose evidence is dropped into a `Bool` (P23) or made twice (P3 with
`validate_graph`); a graph type that admits an impossible triangle (P21); criteria hidden as
constants, which is a function whose type hides a parameter (P28). Luciano's principle (`00`) is
the fix: a check is made once and recorded in the type. The pieces are the rewrite over `VecOf n`,
R11 and `refine`, bounds in types, a domain precondition for `negate`, the triangle law in
`ValidDistanceGraph`, and criteria as arguments of the functions and the structures that use them.

**Root 3. The statistical laws cannot say how likely they are to be wrong, and the project's
diagnostics do not see scale.** P7 and P20, with the prior on sigma of P21. A law that passes with
probability 1 on a wrong model is not evidence, and the certificate does not show the difference.
`08` C8 to C10 (a declared level, one repetition with another seed, power measured on controls) fix
the law side. On the project side, the information that sees a wrong scale is the tape's precision:
a prior on sigma from it, and a diagnostic that compares the fitted sigma with that prior.

**Root 4. The interpreter's cost model.** Budgets that cannot be lowered (P27), not-run laws
recomputed per importing file (P10), flattening with the fuel of a run (P5), two evaluators (P4),
and so certification in hours (P33). The cheap fixes come first: budgets as arguments, known
outcomes reused (C4), a bounded fuel for flattening. The evaluators are a decision (2.3, stage 7).

**Root 5. The dictation protocol produces readings that are ambiguous and unchecked at the time of
measurement.** P16 to P19 and P22. A number said aloud has two readings ("8 metros 5"); a block
with self-corrections contradicts the geometry and nobody notices for a month; a pair is named the
wrong way around; a height carried from the previous position may or may not apply. The fix has a
part in the data (Luciano's review), a part in the types (P21's triangle law catches the class
before any fit) and a part in the next session's protocol (a read-back of each number in two forms,
the geometric check on the spot, one more distance per speaker).

**Root 6. Rules without a mechanism, sessions without a protocol.** P15, P30, P31, P32. The rules
that held through the night were the mechanical ones. The literal lint with a hook after each edit
is the one mechanism that would have caught `lmMaxIter`; `luthier check` must be able to fail on
pending obligations when asked; sessions need file ownership, worktrees, and a certification that
runs in a tree nobody edits.

### 2.3 The stages

Every change to the interpreter invalidates every certificate, so the interpreter work is one round
with one committed recertification at its end (v0.2), and timed uncommitted certifications in
between as measurements. The stages are ordered by dependency; 1 and 2 run in parallel (different
files), 3 needs Luciano's annotations of `08`, 4 to 6 follow, 7 is a decision with its own path.
Tracks II and III run alongside from day one.

### 2.4 The classification of a numeric constant (for P28 and Track II)

A constant in a `.lut` or Python file is one of four things, and the lint of Track II applies this:

- *A criterion*: a number a computation consumes in term position (a tolerance, a budget, a
  threshold, a count of seeds, a start spread). It is an argument: of the function, of a settings
  record the caller passes, of the run (`--set`), or of the law (drawn by the test's binding). A
  library may name a default value, and a program that uses the default says so where it calls.
- *A bound of a claim*: a number in type position, the bound of a refinement (`positionBox`,
  `sigmaLow`, `sigmaHigh`, the bounds of `lmTol`'s type). It stays where it is; it is part of what
  the law claims, and the certificate's reach shows it.
- *A mathematical fact* (`pi`, `rigidMotions = 6`, the three translations and three rotations of a
  rigid body in space): annotated `-- fact:` with the fact.
- *A resolution of the environment* (the grid of an integration, a limit above which a definition
  is not evaluated such as `laplaceLimit`): annotated `-- resolution:` with why it is not a
  criterion.

The lint flags a bare numeric literal in a top-level definition unless it is in type position or
carries one of the two annotations. `nodes : Int := 7` in `distances.lut` is the design the
certification runs on: a bound of the claim, written in the domain type of the laws.

## Part 4. Executive summary

Six problems, in order of importance. For each: what it is, why it happens, what is proposed, and
who does it.

**1. The certificates say more than they test.** A law passes on 24 evaluated cases and the
certificate says 100; the tolerance of an optimizer is tested on (0, 16] and used at 10^-12; a law
over an `Objective` is never run and nothing fails; a statistical law passes with the same
probability on a right model and on a wrong one, and the certificate cannot tell. The cause is one:
the generator is implicit, owned by the type, with structural defaults and budgets fixed in the
code, and the certificate records neither what drew the cases nor how many were evaluated. The
specification of the fix is written (`08-tests.md`, with your decisions of today): generators are
named values and functors over the type, numbers carry their bounds in the type and their metric in
the generator, functions and theories are drawn from a census of the program's own models, drawing
a type is an error, budgets are arguments, and a certificate is keyed by its binding and counts
evaluated cases. Statistical laws declare their level, repeat a failure once and measure their
power on the controls. It is the interpreter round, in my hands, one recertification at the end
(v0.2). It needs your annotations of `08` before the generator syntax is written (stage 3); the
honest-and-cheap part (stage 1) does not wait.

**2. Session A's headline number rests on an impossible measurement.** Sigma 25.7 cm comes from
the block of parlante_A2: 3.35 m to mic2 and 1.13 m to mic3 cannot both hold when mic2 and mic3 are
5.40 m apart, and no relabeling of the three values closes the triangle. Without the block sigma is
4.9 cm. Nothing in the pipeline checks the triangle inequality, and the PIT's uniformity test
cannot see a wrong scale because it uses the fitted sigma. Three more items need your eye: the

were taken in three dimensions. Proposed: your review of the four items and a refit (stage 0); the

constants, which is a function whose type hides a parameter. Your principle settles it: a check is
made once and recorded in the type. Proposed: the libraries rewritten over `VecOf n` (the generic

track in parallel with the interpreter track; R11 after the generators.
