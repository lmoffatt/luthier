## Q1. `data` without recursion

Today 3.R3 and 4.R4 say a `data` must have a recursive constructor, and a non-recursive sum is
written with `< a : A | b : B >` and named by an alias. `grammar.lth` breaks the rule seven times
(`TokenKind`, `Keyword`, `Literal`, `Op`, `Binder`, `Body`, `SurfaceAct`), and the specification
breaks it itself: `Unit` and `Empty` are `data` (3.S7).

Your proposal: a sum is written as a `data` without recursion. Then what makes a `data` is that it
is freely generated by named constructors (distinct constructors, distinct values; each injective),
recursive or not; and 4.R4 keeps only its other half: an anonymous structural type (a product, an
exponential, `< ... >` with no name of its own) names nothing new and is an alias. The anonymous
sum `< a : A | b : B >` stays for the cases where no name is wanted, as `A + B` is `< left | right >`.

Recommended: yes. Changes 3.R3, 3.S7, 4.R4, and the programs `r4_data_nonrecursive.reject.lut`
(it becomes an accept) and `r4_product_named.reject.lut` (unchanged).

## Q2. What a theory file may hold besides laws

12.S23 and L2.6 say a `.lth` holds "types, declarations and laws". `ebnf.lth` also holds
`append`, `rulesIn`, `splits`, `withStart`; `grammar.lth` holds `keywordText`, `opText`,
`tokenView` and the value `luthierSyntax : Ebnf`. GPT reads them as a pattern against 12.S23.

Two readings:

- (a) A law may bring the definitions it needs to be stated, and a theory file may hold a value
  of a type it defines when that value is part of the theory (`luthierSyntax` is the grammar of
  Luthier, the thing the theory is about). What a `.lth` may not hold is what touches the world
  (an extern, an action) or a model of a theory declared elsewhere.
- (b) A `.lth` holds laws only; helpers and values go to a `.lut` the theory imports.

Recommended (a): under (b) almost no law can be written, since a law over lists needs `append`.
The line is "no world, no model of another theory", which is what laziness needs (12.S23). 12.S23
and L2.6 gain a sentence.

## Q3. How a bare term stands at the top level

2.S21 turns `test` and `cite` into functions, but the act grammar of 2.S5 has no act that is a
bare term, so `test f (n <- 100)` at the top level of a file parses as nothing. `grammar.lth`
invented the act `datum` (a bare term kept by the module, D9 of the superseded plan), and GPT
rightly says it is unmarked and belongs in the specification first.

Recommended: 2.S5 gains `act ::= ... | term`, a datum of the module, with 4.R12's rule that no
`<-` stands there; which terms may stand there (a `TestSpec`, a citation) is the checker's.

## Q4. Trailing separators and several names per import

Two places where `luthierSyntax` departs from the letter: chapter 2 allows a trailing `;` after
the last constructor of a `data` and the last arm of a `match`, and `luthierSyntax` does not; and
12.S22 writes one name per import line while `luthierSyntax` allows several
(`import "ebnf.lth" Ebnf Rhs TokenView derives ...`).

Recommended: allow both, and say so in 2.S5, 2.S9 and 12.S22. A trailing separator is harmless and
every editor leaves one; several names per line is the form the files already use, and one name
per line would make `grammar.lth` import eleven times.

## Q5. Positions in the tree

GPT: carry source positions, but outside the structural equality of the tree, or `RoundTrip`
fails since printing moves them. The files carry none in the tree and positions only in errors.

Recommended: none in the tree for now, errors carry them (as the files do). When the checker
needs to point at a term, a position map beside the tree, keyed by the path to the node, keeps
`RoundTrip` as it is. Reversible.

## Q6. `operator` and `quote`

`grammar.lth` keeps `operator` as a keyword (2.S21 said "possibly") because it binds a symbol,
which no function can take as an argument; and makes `quote` a keyword because it captures a term
unevaluated, which 2.S21 does not list. GPT accepts both as marked but notes that as keywords they
cannot name constructors, and the file uses them so (my error, to fix either way).

Recommended: both keywords, 2.S21 updated. Nine keywords in all: `data`, `match`, `if`, `then`,
`else`, `U`, `W`, `operator`, `quote`.
