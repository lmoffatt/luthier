## Q1. `data` without recursion, and what gives its constructors meaning

Today 3.R3 and 4.R4 say a `data` must have a recursive constructor, and a non-recursive sum is
written with `< a : A | b : B >` and named by an alias. `grammar.lth` breaks the rule seven times
(`TokenKind`, `Keyword`, `Literal`, `Op`, `Binder`, `Body`, `SurfaceAct`), the specification breaks
it itself (`Unit` and `Empty` are `data`, 3.S7), and all four reviewers flag it. Claude adds that
3.R2 with 4.R4 makes every nine-case enumeration the same type, a sum of units told by shape.

Your proposal: a sum is written as a `data` without recursion. Your question: what makes such a
`data` meaningful, and where are the laws that justify each constructor?

The answer agreed in chat: a `data` is freely generated by named constructors (distinct
constructors, distinct values; each injective, 3.R3), recursive or not; that freedom says nothing
of what a constructor means. Its meaning is given by an observer with a law: `keywordText` says
`kwData` is the text "data"; the laws of `derives` say what `eps` and `seq` are. So a `data` comes
with the observers that tell its constructors apart and the laws that bind them (G1; the table of
observers against constructors of 4.S19), and 4.R3, carried to `data`, says a constructor no
observer distinguishes means nothing. 4.R4 keeps its other half: an anonymous structural type (a
product, an exponential, `< ... >` with no name of its own) names nothing new and is an alias; the
anonymous sum stays for the cases where no name is wanted.

Marked ok. Changes 3.R3, 3.S7, 4.R4, 4.R3 (observers of a `data`), and the programs
`r4_data_nonrecursive.reject.lut` (becomes an accept, with an observer) and a new reject for a
`data` whose constructor no observer touches.

## Q2. What a theory file may hold besides laws

12.S23 and L2.6 say a `.lth` holds "types, declarations and laws". `ebnf.lth` also holds
`append`, `rulesIn`, `splits`, `withStart`; `grammar.lth` holds `keywordText`, `opText`,
`tokenView` and the value `luthierSyntax : Ebnf`. GPT reads them as a pattern against 12.S23.

Two readings:

- (a) A law may bring the definitions it needs to be stated, and a theory file may hold a value
  of a type it defines when that value is part of the theory (`luthierSyntax` is the grammar of
  Luthier, the thing the theory is about). What a `.lth` may not hold is what touches the world
  (an extern, an action) or a model of a theory declared elsewhere.
- (b) A `.lth` holds laws only; helpers and values go to a `.lut` the theory imports.

Recommended (a): under (b) almost no law can be written, since a law over lists needs `append`.
The line is "no world, no model of another theory", which is what laziness needs (12.S23). 12.S23
and L2.6 gain a sentence.

a

## Q4. Trailing separators and several names per import

Two places where `luthierSyntax` departs from the letter: chapter 2 allows a trailing `;` after
the last constructor of a `data` and the last arm of a `match`, and `luthierSyntax` does not; and
12.S22 writes one name per import line while `luthierSyntax` allows several
(`import "ebnf.lth" Ebnf Rhs TokenView derives ...`).

Recommended: allow both, and say so in 2.S5, 2.S9 and 12.S22. A trailing separator is harmless and
every editor leaves one; several names per line is the form the files already use, and one name
per line would make `grammar.lth` import eleven times.

ok

## Mine to fix, no question

- `withStart` is not fresh and can make the laws inconsistent (GPT's first change): the laws will
  speak of a right-hand side directly, through a declared `derivesRhs`, with `derives e ts =
  derivesRhs e (ref e.start) ts`.
- The laws do not pin the least fixed point (`S ::= S`, `rep eps`): add the induction law, or
  define `derives` by finite derivations with an `exists` over them (D-2026-10-13-l).
- `match` and `operator` used as constructor names; `pName` and `pCon "x" []` are the same text;
  `tuple []`, `lambda []`, `intLit (-1)` are trees that no text gives, so `RoundTrip` quantifies
  over well-formed trees (a subtype) or the tree gets stricter.
- The two-text laws must also assert that one side parses.
- Laws for the tree of each form (declarations, binders, clauses, data bodies, Pi, Sigma, records,
  lambdas, matches, named arguments, `if`, projection, patterns) and for the lexer (names, dotted
  names, numbers, strings).
- `program` requires a `"\n"` after every act; the layout law must say layout gives one.
- 2.S20 says `grammar.lut`; it is `grammar.lth`.
- `derives` defined by a conjunction of laws is a subtype, not the function (DeepSeek, Claude):
  the pattern becomes `Derives : { derives : Ebnf -> Rhs -> List TokenView -> Bool }`, laws over
  `(d : Derives)`, `Derives := laws`, and the model a named value `backtrack : Derives` in its own
  file; `Grammar` and `Recognizer` take their `Derives` as a parameter.
- The domain of the laws and of the model's certificate is the subtype of well-formed grammars
  (no left recursion, Kimi and Claude); with fuel the model answers `false` where the laws force
  `true` (Kimi's `start → seq [start, lit "x"] | eps`), so it is wrong there, not silent.
- `eval` (all four), and the multi-name `field` the tree cannot hold (Kimi); `pName`/`pCon` and
  `record`/`recordLaw` merged (Kimi, Claude); `and`, `or`, `mod` as `Op`s, not applications
  (Claude).
- `lex` and `layout` characterized: the lexer as a second `Ebnf` over characters with its own
  deriving law (Claude), the layout pass with laws of its own including what it does at the end
  of the text and after `{` (Claude, Kimi).
- Several case laws contradict `ParsesWhatDerives` as the grammar stands, since `datum ::= term`
  derives `import "x.lut"` as an application and `f < a : A | b : B >` derives (Claude); `cat
  "name"` admits `and`, `or`, `mod`, `as`, `import`.
- `StartIsRef` is redundant given `RefUnion` (Kimi).
