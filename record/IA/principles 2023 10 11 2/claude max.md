Ordered by how much each blocks the implementation.

**1. The planner's class is defined three ways.** G0 (and the opening of L10): the quotient by the law's equality, one class per mathematical function, representative free. L10 (draft 4) and L21: the component connected by pairwise identity certificates; nothing enters by passing laws, a lone implementation is never replaced. L20: a specialization enters "certified against the same law" and bridges are "coherent up to the law". Under D‑d (laws accumulate and need not determine the function) the first is not even well defined — a law‑class contains several mathematical functions, so "one class per function" and "the representative is free" are false and D‑m is the correction. But G0 is left unchanged and L20 still uses the law‑class notion. The planner needs one admission rule; as written, the guide licenses what D‑m forbids and L20 admits by a route L10 closes. Either reword G0 ("free among the certified‑equivalent") and L20 (a specialization and a bridge each enter through an identity certificate with its distance), or the next reader of "the guide" re‑derives the free planner and reopens D‑m.

**2. Whether an agreement test is a law is answered both ways.** W5 says "agrees with the native" would be a law that copies an implementation, so it is not one — and then calls it "the identity law of L21". L14 says no type with a law, no application. If identity laws count, L14's gate is passed by any extern with a native twin and G2 is bypassed (acquaintance through the back door). If they don't, W5's bootstrap is blocked, and if draft 4's "default for every value" extends the gate to natives, a function with zero characterizing laws cannot run at all — which is where D‑d says every function starts. The holotype of G2 (draft 3) makes the same slip at organizer level: `\x{x*x}` is a term, a specimen "checked against it" is checked by comparing outputs — the oracle under another name — and in L21 the formula is itself a specimen "checked by the same law", i.e. against itself. Separate the proposition (`Sqr x = x * x`, a law over the signature) from the term (a definition, runnable over `Rational`). The analogy is Luciano's, and in nomenclature it runs the other way: the holotype is the name‑bearing specimen and the diagnosis is the text, which makes the native implementation the holotype (W5's original reading) and the law the description — the opposite assignment from G2's.

**3. `assumed` means three things, and W6 rejects one of them.** A kind of hole, "reasoned with and never run" (L8); a grade of certificate (L2, L11); the opt‑out of certification in a demand (L14, D‑n). W6: a law not known to be true is worse than a hole. The language offers as a first‑class grade what the way of working forbids. The implementation needs to know what `assumed` elaborates to, whether an assumed law may sit in the cone of a certificate graded `sampled`, and what a result leaving under the opt‑out carries, given L2's "what leaves carries one". A related order problem: W6 wants laws before the algorithm; G2 says a description is "observed otherwise", after the specimen; L5 warns the observed law may be a transcription. W6's mutation criterion (D‑h) cannot tell them apart — a law transcribed from the implementation is failed by every mutation, so it is maximally diagnostic under W6 and pure acquaintance under G2. The discriminator the draft already has is the holotype idea used correctly: a law stated over the theory's signature cannot transcribe a model's code.

**4. `Cost` already has four composition rules while §4 says the algebra is open.** Sum of the links of a chain (L10); worst cost seen, a max (L2); a grade by distribution and sample size (L11, the lattice of §4); −log p adding under independence (G3, §4). Sum of distances and sum of −log p are different operations on different quantities; one `Cost` with one `+` cannot carry both, and whoever picks the metric reading breaks Bayes, whoever picks Bayes breaks L10's triangle step. Two soundness gaps follow either way: the worst cost seen on a sample is not a bound, yet L10 charges the chain sum against the demanded tolerance as if it were; and L21's "the certificate of the law travels by the certificate of identity, with the observed distance added" is a pointwise triangle inequality, valid only if both were sampled on the same points — which L11 itself says changes the grade. Minimum: two quantities (a distance and an evidence) in every certificate, and the chain step allowed only between certificates over the same sample. L15's "functor in Lawvere's sense" (pending) belongs here: L20 makes a theory a type and a model an inhabitant, not a category and a product‑preserving functor, and the draft itself says rounding is not a homomorphism. "Approximate homomorphism with an additive defect" is what draft 4 argues; say that and drop the word, or `11` inherits a claim it cannot use.

**5. The memo has two keys and `test` has two natures.** L13 (draft 4): the cache key does not depend on the observer. L19 (draft 4): a result is identified by, among other things, the demanded type and tolerance. The demand is the observer; either L13 means a different cache (derivability) and should say so, or it is wrong as written. L14: certificates are paid once through the memo; L19: tests are actions; §4: an action is never served from the memo — so the certificates a `test` demands come from the memo while the `test` itself never may. That split is defensible but unwritten, and the opposite reading, every verification re‑runs, follows just as well from L12 + L19. Three verification regimes share one word: on the values actually computed (L5, per input), sampled over a distribution (L11, per function), bit‑identity with a seed (L21, per implementation); their memo keys differ, and L5 mixes the first two in one sentence. Two extern details: the certificate "stays by hash" of a Luthier declaration that does not change when the Python library does (W10 says the version goes in; L14 does not); and externs are the only reason L18's confluence must be certified rather than assumed, since a host function can hide state the graph does not show — so the extern declaration needs to say definition or action (L12: a read is an action), and nothing asks for it.

**6. Time in a block contradicts L9, and L19 and L16 disagree on what a name means.** L9: time is reconstructed from dependencies, not the text. In a block, `x <- 1; x <- 2; print x` has two independent nodes and only the text says what prints; D‑i confines `<-` to blocks, so L9 holds at the top level of a file and a block is sequential — say so, and weaken "script and interpreter are the same" accordingly. The sharper conflict: `x <- 1; y := f x; x <- 2; print y`. L16 binds y to the term "for all time", so y stays `f 1` (SSA); L19 plans "backward from the actions over names", and over names y reaches the current x, so y becomes `f 2`. One is a notebook with frozen cells, the other is propagation; they are different languages, and the session was implemented on 2026‑10‑11 without the worked example W2 exists to require for exactly this.
