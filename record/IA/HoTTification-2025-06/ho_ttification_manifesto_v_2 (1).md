# 📜 HoTTification Manifesto v1.0

### A Statement of Purpose for the Semantic Elevation of Scientific Inference

---

## 🧭 Mission

To elevate scientific software — particularly inference engines — from empirically functional code to epistemically principled systems. We believe reproducibility, interpretability, and trustworthiness in computational science require not just better tools, but better epistemic foundations.

This manifesto affirms that:

- Verification must become **semantic**, not just syntactic.
- Scientific code should encode its **epistemic structure**, not just its algorithms.
- The correct long-term foundation is type-theoretic, compositional, and homotopical.

---

## 🧬 Philosophical Commitments

- **Reproducibility is a formal property.**
- **Adjunctions are not abstract — they structure diagnostics.**
- **Tests are not patches — they are morphisms.**
- **Safety is not an afterthought — it is a constructible semantic zone.**
- **Scientific software is not engineering — it is executable epistemology.**

---

## 🏗 Programmatic Scaffold (Sketch)

While implementation may vary, our vision unfolds in layered structure:

1. **SAV (Semantic Adjoint Verification)** — smart tests derived from adjunctions
2. **Semantic Interfaces** — DSLs that expose structural contracts
3. **Proof Capsules** — local type-safe wrappers (e.g. `ScientificSafe<T>`)
4. **Contagiously Safe DSLs** — systems where structure is enforced at the compiler level

This is not a waterfall. This is a homotopical program: recursive, incremental, convergent.

---

## 📣 Call to Action

We invite the scientific software community to:
- Think functorially
- Write tests semantically
- Move toward type safety as a *natural consequence* of epistemic clarity
- And ultimately: build systems that do not merely work, but know why they work
