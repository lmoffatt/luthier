The document is internally consistent in most of its cross-references. The contradictions that would bite an implementer cluster in three places: G3 (cost-valued truth) colliding with the equality vocabulary inherited from G0 and G2; a planner allowed to choose among implementations the laws don't distinguish; and several pending decisions written as if settled. In W12 order, most damaging first.

**1. Quotient vs metric: L10, L12, L16 against G3, and L9 already says so.** L9 states that when laws carry a distance the second structure is "a metric space", yet L10 and L12 are written as quotients: "one class per mathematical function, the class chosen by law, the representative by cost." Closeness within a tolerance is not transitive, so there are no classes to choose from: A≈B and B≈C within ε gives A≈C only within 2ε. A planner that substitutes representatives transitively, or chains L20's bridges, exceeds the demanded tolerance unless errors are composed, and the composition algebra is the first open item. The same collision reaches `:=`: L16 opens by separating definition from observational equality, then (draft 3) makes the law of `:=` observational, "a distance under G3". That is the honest consequence of L10 (the bound term may be realized by a cheaper representative), but it means `:=` is no longer a definition in G0's sense and ≡ by reduction does not exist over floats. Underneath, the note invokes two different Lawveres: the subobject classifier Ω of a topos (G0, HoTT) and [0,∞)-enrichment (G3, Lawvere metric spaces). Replacing Ω by [0,∞) leaves no topos; "a type is a proposition" and "a law is a cost" don't share a framework without quantitative or graded type theory, which is research, not a library. The minimal repair is to keep the quotient in the theory (Real), state that models carry a metric plus the demand's tolerance, and anchor every substitution to the law rather than to another representative.

**2. Laws that don't determine the function, plus choice by cost, yields the degenerate representative.** D-2026-10-11-d makes characterization a limit, not a requirement (G2, L5), while L10 keeps "one class per mathematical function" and G1/G3 hand the choice inside the class to cost. If `Sqr` is known only by `sqr x ≥ 0`, the constant zero function is in the class and is the cheapest, and nothing in the principles stops the planner from picking it. The draft-3 holotype clause in G2 half-resolves and half-contradicts this: `\x {x*x}` as "the formula of Sqr" is a defining term, exactly the knowledge "by acquaintance" that G2 says is type theory's limitation, and a specimen "checked against it" makes the formula an oracle, which W5 restated forbids ("agrees with the native" copies an implementation). In nomenclature the holotype is the name-bearing specimen, not the diagnosis; read that way the metaphor says there is a reference implementation, read as diagnosis it says there are only laws. The certifier is built differently in each case, and for functions with no formula in the signature (sin, a solver, a sampled integral) only the second reading exists, where under-determination is unmitigated. A rule is needed: a representative may replace another only inside a class the laws actually determine, or only when a pairwise equivalence law exists, which is W5's consistency check made legitimate as a law between two specimens.

**3. G3 is a candidate by the document's own process and load-bearing by its own derivation.** The P13 check gives it one decision, W2 says no rule enters without a worked example, and the worked examples for G3 (sum of n floats, κ(A), the sampled probability) are listed as open. Yet seven L's derive from it (L9, L10, L11, L15, L16, L17, L21) and §1 makes G0's quotient and G2's inhabitance depend on it. W3's "start minimal" cannot be followed either way: the minimal version is Boolean, and the Boolean versions of L10, L16 and L21 are different rules (exact classes, bit-identity, `Maybe` constructors), so the spec written now is rewritten when G3 lands, the destabilization W13 warns against. Inside G3 there are two incompatible algebras: −log p adds under independence; distances compose through Lipschitz constants, multiplicatively. And rounding Real→Float64 is not Lipschitz at all: it is a step function, so two reals astride a rounding boundary map to distinct floats at arbitrarily small distance. L15's "expands distances by at most a constant" is false as stated; rounding is non-expansive with an additive defect, so the cost algebra needs an additive term as well as a multiplicative one before any certificate can be composed.

**4. "Verified before constructed" vs "verifying is demanding".** L2 (membership is a guarantee), L3 (one act), L4 (types are verified before they are constructed) and L14 (validated the first time an action asks for it, not applied without a law) all speak as if certification precedes use. L19 says tests are actions, that an action asks for "the value … its test" as separate things, and that what never reaches an output needs no certificate. So a value can reach `print` certified by nothing, and L2's guarantee holds only when a test was separately demanded; L14 and L19 give different defaults for the same event. One rule is needed: either a value demand pulls every certificate in its cone (every print runs tests, at a cost), or membership is a lazily checked claim and L2–L4 are reworded.

**5. The memo key is too small for what now feeds a result.** L19 memoizes by hash of code, inputs and mode (proposed by the model, accepted). By draft 3 a result also depends on the demanded type and tolerance (G1), on which laws the demand selected and how much history the certificate kept (L13), on the model threaded down from the action (L20), on the place of execution (L10), on seed and sampled distribution (L11, W10), and on the state of the run database the planner consults (G3, L10). Two consequences. G3's "kept across runs" and L19's "each node once" contradict each other for sampled certificates: either a certificate is immutable under its hash and evidence never accumulates, or the seed is in the key and the same node is recomputed, which G3 wants and L19 forbids. And L18 forbids "what the graph does not show", which is exactly the planner's dependence on past runs: the same program and inputs can yield a different representative, hence a different error, depending on what ran before. W10/W11 cannot reproduce such a result unless the plan is itself an input by hash. `plan.lut` would provide that, but it is pending, so today the planner is an unnamed patch (W9) that defines semantics from Python, against W1 and W5.

**6. Pending decisions written as settled.**
- `<-` and blocks: "after `<-` until the next `<-`" is textual order, so inside a block statements do not commute (L9). The document keeps L9 by banning `<-` at the top level, but L19 also says "script and interpreter are the same" while the session is defined by `<-`; both cannot hold with the ban. If `<-` desugars to L9's own before/after variables, the ban is unnecessary; if not, L9 is scoped, not universal. The spec already has blocks; the decision is marked pending.
- Which model a function runs in (L20): resolved from scope, passed explicitly, named by the argument's type (D-2026-10-11-j), or threaded down from the action (G1, L13). Four mechanisms, no precedence. A `Z7` argument in a scope whose model is `Int`, under an action demanding `Float64`, is the W1 counterexample waiting to happen.
- Types by construction: L4 defers them, L20 puts `data`, the recursion principle and β/η in the kernel now, and §4 admits η is not statable. The one part of the language that is by construction is the one part whose laws cannot be stated by verification, so L7's hand-executable primitives have no checkable theory yet.

**7. Smaller inconsistencies.**
- G4 "generates no L of its own", but L10 lists G4 among its sources.
- The terminology header says modules are namespaces "until modules are values"; L20 says "modules are values".
- An object is "the cycle of a type and its laws" while L9's DAG has no cycles; the unrolling (material type → law → certified type) belongs where the term is defined.
- L5 picks cases by "the generators the law's theory dictates (three elements for associativity)"; L11 says the grade depends on the distribution sampled. Two case-selection rules, and "three elements" is the arity of the law, not a generating set of any theory; over floats, associativity fails, so the generator rule yields no grade.
- `proved` by citation with `checked: none` is W6's "law not known to be true" wearing the top grade; and a theorem about Real proves nothing about Float64, so grades must attach to (law, model), not to the law.
- L17 bans constants, so every action must carry its tolerance, and L10's "expected loss" needs a loss function the demand never mentions; both need declared defaults or explicit fields.

Under W15, three of these are conflicts between principles to be ordered by a decision rather than analysed further: G3 against G0/G2 on what equality is (1, 2); G1's laziness against L2's guarantee (4); L19's memoization against G3's accumulation (5). The rest are ambiguities a worked example (W2) would expose on its own.
