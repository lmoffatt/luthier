A bibliographic search of 2026-10-09, for the record and for Luciano. It follows `02-que-robar.md`
(certificates, gradual verification, the laws of type classes) and the chat of 2026-10-08 and
2026-10-09 (`README.md` here: how a program is executed, and laws valued in something other than

How it was made. Eight searches ran in parallel, one per aspect, each with a brief describing the
relevant part of Luthier and a list of what to leave out (chat:b377c2a0). Each search checked its
references against the publisher or Crossref. Afterwards every DOI was resolved again with

Who wrote what. The citations are the sources. The line under each one, what Luthier would take from
it, and the sentence that closes each section, what the search did not find, are proposals of the
models that ran the search, edited by the model that assembled this document. A "not found" means

## The reading in one screen

Every piece of Luthier has a strong precedent, and several near misses are close enough that
Luthier has to say what it adds:

- PVS predicate subtypes (`rushby1998`) are `{ x : T | p }` as the central element, with proof
  obligations where Luthier has tests.
- PetaBricks (`ansel2009`) puts several implementations of one concept in the source and lets an
  autotuner choose, without laws or a posterior.
- Darulova and Kuncak (`darulova2014`) write programs over an ideal `Real` and compile to a
  realization proven to meet an accuracy specification.
- Bishop and Povyakalo (`bishop2017`) publish the transfer of a reliability bound between
  operational profiles, `p_V ≤ c·p_U`, in frequentist form over discrete classes.
- Strigini and Povyakalo (`strigini2013`) give the prior with an atom at zero failure probability,
  which is how a proved or exhaustive grade would feed a sampled posterior.
- Chapman, Altenkirch and Kaposi, and MetaCoq (`chapman2009`, `altenkirch2016`, `sozeau2020`) define
  a type theory's syntax, typing and conversion laws inside a type theory.
- Hinsen (`hinsen2018`) argues for a digital scientific notation, executable and readable by the
  scientist, kept apart from the software that runs it.

What the eight searches did not find, and where Luthier could say something new:

1. Several implementations certified as models of one theory defined only by its laws, with their
   agreement a certified law and its tolerance derived from their error bounds (sections 1, 3, 6).
2. A stored posterior over the behaviour of a model, with each certificate a decision for a Use and
   transfer between Uses in Bayesian form (sections 2 and 7).
3. Two traces compared by consequence and by cost, the cost a partial order measured with its
   variance (sections 5 and 7).
4. A self-interpreter whose reduction rules are laws in the language, with the evaluation strategy a
   parameter, certified by lock-step comparison of machine states against an independent host,
   and self-modification ruled out by design (section 4).
5. A record, inside the program's history, of which specification text came from the human and
   which from the model (section 8).

Five results that bear on decisions already taken:

- Knight and Leveson (`knight1986`): independently written versions fail together far more often
  than independence predicts. Agreement between a native model and an extern one is weaker evidence
  than it looks, more so when one model wrote both.
- Kaufman and others (`kaufman2026`): programmers judged correct LLM-written assertions right 74% of
  the time and incorrect ones 49%, with the same confidence, and explanations did not help. This is
  the tension between O3 (models work at length) and O2 (membership in a type is a certificate),
  measured.
- Littlewood and Strigini (`littlewood1993`), with the rule of three (`hanley1983`): a failure
  probability of 1e-9 cannot be certified by testing alone. The prior does the work, so it has to be
  explicit in the certificate.
- Reynolds (`reynolds1972`): a metacircular interpreter inherits the evaluation order of its host
  unless it is written in continuation-passing style. That matters for making the strategy a
  parameter.
- Rule and others (`rule2018`): of over a million notebooks on GitHub, one in four had no
  explanatory text at all, and in academic notebooks a minority discussed reasoning or results. A
  text field in a definition will often stay empty unless something asks for it.

## 1. Theories as types, models as values

Luthier's central element is the subtype by law, `{ x : T | p }`, and a theory is a signature cut by
laws whose inhabitants are its models (`docs/guide/00-fundamental-elements.md`, *Subtype by law*).
Algebraic specification has studied this for fifty years with proofs; refinement and predicate
subtypes put the same set in a type system, again with proofs or decision procedures. Luthier keeps
the semantics loose (any inhabitant that passes is a model) and replaces proof obligations by tests.

- `goguen1992`. Goguen, Burstall. Institutions: abstract model theory for specification and programming. JACM 39(1):95-146, 1992. https://doi.org/10.1145/147508.147524
  Institution = signatures, sentences, models, satisfaction invariant under change of notation; Exp over NumberModel read as a theory morphism.

- `sannella2012`. Sannella, Tarlecki. Foundations of Algebraic Specification and Formal Software Development. Springer EATCS, 2012. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-17336-3
  Standard survey; initial vs loose semantics; Luthier's laws are loose (any passing inhabitant is a model).

- `goguen1977`. Goguen, Thatcher, Wagner, Wright. Initial algebra semantics and continuous algebras. JACM 24(1):68-95, 1977. https://doi.org/10.1145/321992.321997
  The initial alternative to loose semantics.

- `astesiano2002`. Astesiano, Bidoit, Kirchner, Krieg-Brückner, Mosses, Sannella, Tarlecki. CASL: the Common Algebraic Specification Language. TCS 286(2):153-196, 2002. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-3975(01)00368-1
  Structuring operations (union, then, renaming, hiding, free, views) as checklist for building theories over theories.

- `farmer1992`. Farmer, Guttman, Thayer. Little theories. CADE-11, LNCS 607:567-581, 1992. https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-55602-8_192
  Many small theories joined by interpretations; a law certified once transfers along interpretations.

- `rabe2013`. Rabe, Kohlhase. A scalable module system. Information and Computation 230:1-54, 2013. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ic.2013.06.001
  MMT theory graphs, edges (includes, views) as checkable values.

- `carette2012`. Carette, O'Connor. Theory presentation combinators. CICM 2012, LNCS 7362:202-215. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-31374-5_14
  Extend/rename/combine; diamond of two theories over a shared base (Exp and others over NumberModel).

- `cohen2020`. Cohen, Sakaguchi, Tassi. Hierarchy Builder: algebraic hierarchies made easy in Coq with Elpi. FSCD 2020, LIPIcs 167:34. https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.FSCD.2020.34
  Factories; what must be automated so theory-over-theory doesn't break clients.

- `garillot2009`. Garillot, Gonthier, Mahboubi, Rideau. Packaging mathematical structures. TPHOLs 2009, LNCS 5674:327-342. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-03359-9_23
  Packed classes.

- `spitters2011`. Spitters, van der Weegen. Type classes for mathematics in type theory. MSCS 21(4):795-825, 2011. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0960129511000119
  Unbundled: carrier and ops as parameters, laws a separate predicate; matches signature/laws split.

- `pollack2000`. Pollack. Dependently typed records for representing mathematical structure. TPHOLs 2000, LNCS 1869:462-479. https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-44659-1_29
  Structures as Σ-types with manifest fields: theory over a fixed model of another.

- `macqueen1986`. MacQueen. Using dependent types to express modular structure. POPL 1986:277-286. https://doi.org/10.1145/512644.512670
  Signatures as Σ-types, structures as inhabitants: theories as types, models as values.

- `rushby1998`. Rushby, Owre, Shankar. Subtypes for specifications: predicate subtyping in PVS. IEEE TSE 24(9):709-720, 1998. https://doi.org/10.1109/32.713327
  Closest precedent of {x:T|p} as central; PVS generates proof obligations, Luthier tests them.

- `freeman1991`. Freeman, Pfenning. Refinement types for ML. PLDI 1991:268-277. https://doi.org/10.1145/113445.113468
  Refinement = subset with no new terms; certificate erased, value kept.

- `rondon2008`. Rondon, Kawaguchi, Jhala. Liquid types. PLDI 2008:159-169. https://doi.org/10.1145/1375581.1375602
  Decidable predicates checked by SMT: where tested laws could be upgraded.

- `jhala2021`. Jhala, Vazou. Refinement types: a tutorial. FnT Programming Languages 6(3-4):159-317, 2021. https://doi.org/10.1561/2500000032
  Best survey of the {x:T|p} side.

- `beth1953`. Beth. On Padoa's method in the theory of definition. Indagationes Math. 15:330-339, 1953. https://doi.org/10.1016/S1385-7258(53)50042-3
  Implicit definability implies explicit: criterion for when laws define one type up to iso (P10) vs a family of models.

Not found by the search: No loose-semantics theory with models certified by tests where the certificate is an erased Σ component; no use of Beth definability as a PL design rule; MMT/MathScheme closest on theories over theories but not executable.

## 2. Testing as statistical evidence

The Bayesian reading of a sampled certificate (D-2026-10-08-a; GPT's second reply in
`record/IA/2026 10 08 170119 GPT.md`) has a long history in software reliability. The Use, the
TestPlan, the zero-failure bound, the prior that has to do the work, and the transfer between
operational profiles all appear there, mostly in frequentist form or over discrete demand classes.

- `miller1992`. Miller, Morell, Noonan, Park, Nicol, Murrill, Voas. Estimating the probability of failure when testing reveals no failures. IEEE TSE 18(1):33-43, 1992. https://doi.org/10.1109/32.120314
  Beta posterior after n failure-free cases, with correction when test distribution differs from operational: earliest Use/TestPlan split.

- `littlewood1993`. Littlewood, Strigini. Validation of ultrahigh dependability for software-based systems. CACM 36(11):69-80, 1993. https://doi.org/10.1145/163359.163373
  Θ<1e-9 cannot come from testing alone; prior must do the work, explicit in certificate.

- `littlewood1997`. Littlewood, Wright. Some conservative stopping rules for the operational testing of safety critical software. IEEE TSE 23(11):673-683, 1997. https://doi.org/10.1109/32.637384
  Bayesian stopping rules; what a failure-and-fix does to the evidence.

- `bishop2011`. Bishop, Bloomfield, Littlewood, Povyakalo, Wright. Toward a formalism for conservative claims about the dependability of software-based systems. IEEE TSE 37(5):708-717, 2011. https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.2010.67
  Conservative Bayesian inference: worst-case prior consistent with partial prior knowledge.

- `strigini2013`. Strigini, Povyakalo. Software fault-freeness and reliability predictions. SAFECOMP 2013, LNCS 8153:106-117. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-40793-2_10
  Prior with atom at θ=0 (perfection): how proved/exhaustive/assumed grades feed a sampled posterior.

- `bishop2017`. Bishop, Povyakalo. Deriving a frequentist conservative confidence bound for probability of failure per demand for systems with different operational and test profiles. RESS 158:246-253, 2017. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ress.2016.08.019
  Bound for new profile = old × max p/p̂: Luthier's transfer p_V ≤ c·p_U already published, frequentist and discrete.

- `musa1993`. Musa. Operational profiles in software-reliability engineering. IEEE Software 10(2):14-32, 1993. https://doi.org/10.1109/52.199724
  How to build the distribution part of a Use.

- `duran1984`. Duran, Ntafos. An evaluation of random testing. IEEE TSE SE-10(4):438-444, 1984. https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.1984.5010257
  Random testing supports a reliability estimate; partition/boundary plans need a model.

- `hamlet1994`. Hamlet. Random testing. Encyclopedia of Software Engineering, Wiley, 1994 (online 2002). https://doi.org/10.1002/0471028959.sof268
  Only profile-distributed testing yields a probability claim.

- `younes2002`. Younes, Simmons. Probabilistic verification of discrete event systems using acceptance sampling. CAV 2002, LNCS 2404:223-235. https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-45657-0_17
  SPRT with α, β: template for statistical laws with level and power.

- `jha2009`. Jha, Clarke, Langmead, Legay, Platzer, Zuliani. A Bayesian approach to model checking biological systems. CMSB 2009, LNCS 5688:218-234. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-03845-7_15
  Beta prior on satisfaction probability, Bayes-factor stopping: same Beta(a,b+n) update.

- `agha2018`. Agha, Palmskog. A survey of statistical model checking. ACM TOMACS 28(1):6, 2018. https://doi.org/10.1145/3158668
  Survey: sample-size bounds, sequential, Bayesian, rare-event IS.

- `corso2021`. Corso, Moss, Koren, Lee, Kochenderfer. A survey of algorithms for black-box safety validation of cyber-physical systems. JAIR 72, 2021. https://doi.org/10.1613/jair.1.12716
  Falsification vs most-likely failure vs failure-probability estimation = TestPlan vs Use.

- `okelly2018`. O'Kelly, Sinha, Namkoong, Tedrake, Duchi. Scalable end-to-end autonomous vehicle testing via rare-event simulation. NeurIPS 2018. https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2018/hash/653c579e3f9ba5c03f2f2f8cf4512b39-Abstract.html

- `moss2024`. Moss, Kochenderfer, Gariel, Dubois. Bayesian safety validation for failure probability estimation of black-box systems. J. Aerospace Information Systems 21(7):533-546, 2024. https://doi.org/10.2514/1.I011395

- `berger1988`. Berger, Wolpert. The Likelihood Principle, 2nd ed. IMS Lecture Notes 6, 1988. https://doi.org/10.1214/lnms/1215466210

## 4. Interpreters and self-description

The aim of writing Luthier's specification and interpreter in Luthier (D-2026-10-08-c), with a
minimal executable kernel, the evaluation strategy as a parameter, evaluation modes as folds over
`Term`, certification of the self-interpreter against the Python host, and self-hacking ruled out.

not repeated.
