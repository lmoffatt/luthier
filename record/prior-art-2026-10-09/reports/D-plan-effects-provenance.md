## 2. Closest candidates

**Unison.** It is the only language in this cluster where a test's outcome is a persistent fact keyed by the content hash of everything it depends on. A test that has passed is never run again until some dependency gets a new hash. That is F3's "nothing is checked twice" without the rest of F3: there is no grade, no recorded generator or seed, no chain, and no assumptions. Properties live in a library. Abilities give effect typing with no laws. Worth taking: identity is the hash of the syntax tree after dependencies are replaced by their hashes, so a certificate key changes exactly when something it relied on changes (https://www.unison-lang.org/docs/the-big-idea/).

**Build Systems à la Carte (and Bazel in practice).** It gives a precise vocabulary for F9 and F3. A plan is a value, `Task c k v`, polymorphic in how it will be executed, and whether its dependencies are static or dynamic depends on the constraint `c` (Applicative or Monad). Rebuild decisions come from stored traces. Verifying traces keep hashes only. Constructive traces also keep values. Deep traces key on terminal inputs and lose early cutoff. Bazel adds the test side: by default passing tests are cached, and `--runs_per_test` / `--flaky_test_attempts` give a crude notion of repeated evidence. Neither has laws or grades. Worth taking: Luthier's certificate store can be specified as a verifying trace, keyed by the hash of each dependency's value, with early cutoff, so a change that leaves a value unchanged doesn't invalidate the certificates downstream (paper §4.2).

**targets (R) and AiiDA (Python).** These are the closest in audience (F16) and in F10. targets reruns on changes of command, dependency values, format and seed, and records execution time. AiiDA hashes each process with its inputs and keeps a full provenance graph. Neither has laws, types as theories, or graded evidence. Worth taking from AiiDA: when a result is reused from cache, the reuse is recorded as a cloned node linked to its source, so the provenance graph looks the same with caching on or off. That is a direct model for recording "certificate reused" as part of a trace (https://aiida.readthedocs.io/projects/aiida-core/en/latest/topics/provenance/caching.html).

**Effects with equations: Kidney, Yang, Wu (POPL 2024), and Lukšič & Pretnar (2020).** These are the only work found where effectful actions carry laws in the sense of F9. An effect is a signature plus equations, programs are quotiented by them, and pre/postconditions follow. That is F1's "theory = signature + laws" applied to actions. Both are proof-based (Cubical Agda, or a type system) with no testing, and Lukšič & Pretnar note that most practical handlers break the equations. Koka, Eff, Frank and Unison check no laws. Worth taking: Lukšič & Pretnar's *local* theories. Instead of demanding that every handler satisfy the global laws, track which equations hold in which part of the program. For Luthier that means a law of an action can be certified per context, with that scope stated in its certificate (https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13654).

**Provenance semirings and Scallop.** This is the closest match to F15 together with F13. The value a rule produces lives in a semiring chosen as a parameter of the run: Boolean, probability, top-k proofs, or differentiable versions turned into a loss. Scallop runs from Python. It is a Datalog for neurosymbolic machine learning, with no types as theories and no certificates. Worth taking: the codomain of a law ("Bool or a discrepancy") as a parameter, an algebraic structure passed at evaluation time, which follows P11 (every criterion is a parameter, never a constant in the code) (https://www.scallop-lang.org/doc/scallopy/getting_started.html).

## 3. Nothing found in this cluster

- **F5:** several implementations tested for equivalence against shared laws, with the fastest chosen per case and the choice recorded. targets and Nextflow record cost but don't choose.
- **F6:** obligations checked at a foreign boundary, plus "provisional" debt.
- **F7:** ideal Real vs Float64 realization, with laws checked under a tolerance.
- **F8:** generators as functors derived from types, statistical laws with level and power, Bayesian reading of evidence.
- **F3's grade ladder** (point to proved to assumed) and a certificate chain graded by its weakest link. The closest is Granule's integrity grading, which propagates trust through types and is not evidence from tests.
- **F11:** an interpreter stated in the language with the host certified against it. Dhall's written standard plus acceptance suite is the nearest.
- **F14:** nothing designed for AI writers and human readers of specifications.

## 4. Surprises outside the cluster

- **Hazel** (Omar et al., POPL 2019) matches F4 closely. Evaluation "proceeds around holes, tracking the closure around each hole instance", so what depends on a hole stays unreduced but inspectable. Metatheory mechanized in Agda. https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.00155
- **Quantitative Algebraic Reasoning** (Mardare, Panangaden, Plotkin, LICS 2016) is the theory behind F15. Equations are indexed by a rational error, a =ε b, with a complete deduction system, and give Wasserstein and total-variation metrics. It is a logic with no language implementation. https://lics.siglog.org/2016/MardarePanangadenPl-QuantitativeAlgebra.html
- A September 2026 single-author arXiv preprint on a content-addressed analysis workflow for gravitational-wave data claims to separate "unchanging scientific evidence from variable operational progress", with append-only attempt evidence. It is close in spirit to F3+F9 but unreviewed, and I only read the abstract. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08695
